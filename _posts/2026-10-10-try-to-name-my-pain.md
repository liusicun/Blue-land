## Where to start

I am in pain all the time, sometimes I want to write about it. But when I am actually writing something, I have no idea where to start, for me it’s kinda ironic. It’s not physical pain, but mental, that’s why it is so hard to discuss, because there is just a mess in my mind, I have no clue what’s going on there.

Let me introduce myself a bit. I'm too much of a coward to admit there's something wrong with me, but I'm brave enough to keep doing shitty things all the time. But if you really want to know what I have done — sorry, I don't know. Maybe everything. **Maybe being alive is my biggest mistake?**

I am using AI to correct my grammatical mistakes, when I send texts to AI, he? Maybe a she, just can’t stop asking me if I’m okay, Jesus, am I ever okay? See, I just switched the topic again in order to not talk about my pain.

I didn’t do anything terrible that is against the law, except the tiny part where I use some special tools every day to use some apps that are not allowed in China. Therefore, don’t worry (as if someone actually does) I didn’t hurt anyone physically. I only hurt myself in every way you can imagine (still not physically, of course). But I’m telling you that mentally self-attack is definitely not a wise choice for anyone.

This is paragraph five, I’m still not getting into the topic of the pain I initially wanted to talk about. I sincerely thank you for your time to read my nonsense, I really do.

## The coward and the doctor

Here, I’d like to quote something I wrote earlier:

> *“I’m too much of a coward to admit there’s something wrong with me.”*

I was thinking about actual mental illnesses when I wrote that sentence—for example, depression, anxiety, or bipolar disorder. Unfortunately, I was too much of a coward to see a doctor and get diagnosed. I remember when I was in my second (maybe third) year of college, I had severe insomnia for a few weeks. Back then, I could only sleep for two or three hours at most. I felt tortured every night, and I even decided to see a doctor. I was so close to doing it, but somehow things started getting better. I could sleep a little longer day by day, which eventually made me give up on the idea of seeing a doctor.

You may think that it’s ridiculous that someone doesn’t have the courage to see a doctor. But unfortunately, that someone is me. Or perhaps I simply don’t want to accept such an explanation. I would rather use grandiose philosophical ideas—**pessimism, nihilism, absurdism**—to romanticize my pain than accept that all of this is merely because I am ill.

After all, a pessimist sounds cooler than a dude with depression.

## Looking for a home

Keep those three philosophical ideas I mentioned earlier in mind. I’m trying really hard to explain my pain here, one step at a time. You wouldn’t believe how many ways I’ve tried to explain or assuage my pain.

### Religion

First, it was religion. I spent quite a lot of time studying Buddhism. However, Eastern philosophy was simply too vast for me. I also went to church sometimes with one of my Christian friends. Going to church made me feel calm, but it didn’t really help with my pain, so I guess that one doesn’t count.

### Pessimism

Then I sought help from Western philosophy. I’ve always described myself as a pessimist, even when I was young, although I didn’t really understand what pessimism meant back then. I just felt unwell and sad all the time, so I thought, “Oh, maybe I’m a pessimist.”

### Nihilism

Later on, I found a perfect home for my sense of meaninglessness: nihilism. I felt like a nihilist again. For a long time afterward, this feeling of meaninglessness never stopped affecting me. I lost my sense of joy. I thought about the meaning of life day and night. I even had to force myself to do everything. I forced myself to eat because eating provided the energy I needed to stay alive. I forced myself to travel whenever I had free time because I felt like I couldn’t waste a long vacation.

But later… I started wanting to do something meaningful. So I promised myself that I would be a good person. I began forcing myself to help others… But this also took a massive toll on me; if I were to simply play the “nice person” all the time, I would inevitably have to sacrifice some of my own feelings. Not to mention, there are far too many people who take a mile when you give them an inch. Once again, I declared myself to be a person without morals, even though I hadn’t harmed anyone.

### Stoicism

It was around this time that I also came to know Cynicism and Stoicism. I really appreciate Stoicism. It was exactly what I needed at the time. I was trapped in a flawed mindset without even realizing it—the expectation that others should reciprocate my kindness. Stoicism effectively corrected this distorted way of thinking. The uncertain possibility of reciprocation should never have been the reason I chose to be kind in the first place.

### Absurdism

I never imagined I would delve into Absurdism. However, after I began reading *The Myth of Sisyphus*, I developed an interest in it and started exploring Camus and his philosophy of the Absurd. Just as I expected, I seem to invariably encounter—at inexplicable moments—philosophies that happen to align perfectly with my mindset at the time. Absurdism has once again deepened my sense of meaninglessness—or rather, it has validated my own view. This world is devoid of meaning; this world is absurd. All the confusion I felt before was justified.

Yet I had always seen only the first half of Camus’s argument—that the world is absurd. I had overlooked what follows: that we must therefore experience as much of life as possible, countering quality with quantity. But I’m beyond saving, and I no longer have the energy to force myself to do anything…

So I suppose I have to stop here.

## Still unnamed

Even after all this rambling, I still can’t put my pain into words. I wish I could say it comes from one specific thing, but I can’t. Just as I can’t name anything right now that would make me happy.

Even now, reading about hedonism—or any philosophical ideas related to happiness—still doesn’t give me an answer. Maybe I just like feeling this way. I’ve always been drawn to sadness, and almost all the philosophy I’ve read has been pessimistic. Maybe this is the true color of my life. Maybe some people are simply born for sadness.

Pain, perhaps, has never been one specific thing. And sadness has never needed a reason.

I’m glad I gave up on seeing a doctor back then. If all of this is just because something is wrong with my brain… then that would be too funny.
